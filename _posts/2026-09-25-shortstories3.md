I felt embarrassed.

I generally prefer not to order from a walk-up window for various reasons. I actually find it nice to order by going inside the café. Even though I sometimes feel insecure about my pronunciation and vocabulary, it feels nice to be inside the café and order my coffee. Sometimes it gives me nice smile to see how they write my name. Also, while waiting for coffee, I love to observe people. Yes, I do plenty of people watching in my free time.

As usual, I ordered a café au lait and some pastries. I was not sure how to pay, so I asked, and the barista pointed me to a screen. A tipping option appeared even before the actual total price for the food was displayed. I disliked this concept, so I did not leave a tip and he watched me do it. I was perfectly fine with that. 

I stood on one corner and started enjoying the vibes of this coffee shop. The shop was crowded, a few people were waiting for their coffee, and names were being called out loud. Most of the tables were occupied. Everyone inside was busy making coffee, taking orders, or calling out names. Outside, I could see a long queue of cars waiting for their turn at the drive-thru window.

I saw people in their 40s enjoying themselves. I thought to myself that cafés feel so much better when people are actually drinking coffee and chatting, instead of college kids doing homework or staring at screens. Sometimes, I wonder if they actually do any serious work or if they are just enjoying the online environment.

Then, my name was called. I grabbed my pastries, but I did not see my coffee, so sat on one of the random tables. The same barista saw me and told me that he was working on it. 

I expected it might be delayed, or something along those lines, and I was mentally ready for it. I was in the middle of a long drive and needed a break, but the option to tip before even seeing the actual price, combined with their suggested tip options, had pissed me off. 

I am happy to leave a tip, and it usually makes me feel good to do so. However, these technological strategies frustrate me because of how tips are now being collected. I believe a tip should be given freely out of goodwill and happiness. Instead, it often feels like it is being forced out of you, regardless of whether you are actually willing to give it. 

I respect the effort made by the workers, and I deeply appreciate the concept of tipping. But when smart programmers prioritize the wrong things, I feel like the true essence of tipping is lost. For instance, I once visited a place where a robot made and served the coffee. To order, I had to use their dedicated app/webpage, and it asked for a tip. Who am I supposed to be tipping? The machine? The software developer who programmed it? Or the coffee business itself? 

If that tip actually makes its way back to the farmers who worked hard to grow and roast the coffee beans, I would happily tip more than 30%. If not, why should I pay extra for a programer ? The owner of that business surely already paid for the software or programmer.
I am not implying that the barista was a robot, and I don't harbor any ill will toward him. I respect him and his work. I was simply sad by the program and corporate house dictating me on how tips are collected. My frustration was with the strategy of the corporate house or the programmar whoever made it.

I was lost on my thoughts, long drive had made me tired. I wasn't looking at my phone. I was just sitting there quietly.

I felt so embarrassed when he opened the cabinet door and walked all the way to my table just to hand-deliver my coffee. I was speechless, and all I could utter was,  "You didn't have to do this. I really appreciate it." I felt like hugging him, at that time. No amount of tip would be enough for that generosity. 

There is an old saying, I think it is related to Buddhism, that if someone slaps your cheek, you should turn the other side to them too. The idea is to let them hit you over and over without fighting back. Until eventually they realize their own cruelty on how bad human being they are being.

Now, I am driving back to my route. 
I am constantly thinking: Was he Buddha? Why did he even bother bringing the coffee to my table? I have never seen anyone do that, especially in a busy coffee shop. What was going through his mind? Was he thinking, "Okay, it's fine if he didn't tip, but let's cherish this moment by giving him extra care and support"?
